How Team of 6 works

Six players compete as three doubles pairs in one continuous relay. The score carries forward when each new pair enters.

1

Pair 1

0 → 10

First pair plays until either team reaches 10.

→
2

Pair 2

10 → 20

New pairs enter. The existing score carries forward.

→
3

Pair 3

20 → 31

Final pairs continue the same score. First legal 31 wins.

Winning point
Point 31 must be won while serving.

No win-by-two. Once your team reaches 30, receiving and winning gives you the serve—but not Point 31.

Below 30Win rally → +1 point
Your team is at 30Receive + win → serve, no point
Your team is at 30Serve + win → 31 → WIN
At 30–30Both teams are frozen

Odd / Even court positions

EVEN / RIGHTServes when team score is even
ODD / LEFTServes when team score is odd

No switching: players stay on their declared court side throughout their relay stage. Only the server changes based on the team's score.

What happens at the 10- and 20-point relay changes?

When either team reaches 10 or 20, both active pairs leave. The next pairs have a maximum 60 seconds to enter. No warm-up rally is played. The team that won the checkpoint rally serves after the change. The incoming server is determined by the team's score: even = right/even player; odd = left/odd player.

Example
Team A reaches 10–7 by winning the rally. Pair 2 enters with Team A serving. Because Team A has 10 (an even score), its right/even player serves.
What exactly is rally scoring and the freeze?

Rally scoring: before a team reaches 30, the winner of every rally earns one point whether serving or receiving.

Freeze: once a team reaches 30, that team cannot score while receiving. It must be serving and win the rally to score Point 31.

30–27
Team A is receiving and wins the rally. The score stays 30–27, but Team A gains the serve. If Team A wins the next rally while serving, it wins 31–27.
If Team A faults at 30–27: Team B is still below 30, so Team B earns a point. The score becomes 30–28. Team B may continue scoring normally until it reaches 30.
30–30
Both teams are frozen. Win while receiving = gain serve, no point. Win while serving = Point 31 and match win.
Player positioning, end change, timeout and lineup

Fixed sides: right/even and left/odd players do not switch during their relay stage.

Change ends: teams change court ends when the first team reaches 16. Odd/even roles remain attached to the player after changing ends.

Timeout: each team has one 60-second timeout for the entire Team of 6 match.

Lineup lock: before the match, each team declares all six players, Pair 1–3 order, and each player's Odd/Even designation. Each player may appear in only one relay stage per match. The lineup is locked once submitted unless Match Control approves a verified medical/safety replacement.

How does Team of 6 progress to the medals?

All four teams play a single round robin. All four advance to seeded semifinals: #1 vs #4 and #2 vs #3. Winners play for the Championship; semifinal losers play for Bronze.

Before Every Match

What happens from the moment your match is called until the first serve.

1
Report when your match is calledYou have 5 minutes once your match is called and the court is ready. After that the match is forfeited, unless the delay was caused by Match Control or another court running late.
2
Conduct the coin tossThe choices depend on your group, shown below.
3
Declare lineup if requiredTeam of 6 lineups include Pair 1–3 order plus Odd/Even designation for every player.
4
Confirm and beginConfirm starting positions, serve and court end, then begin the match.

Coin toss

ACTIVE & LEISUREWinner picks one of two

First serve or starting court end. The other team gets the remaining choice.

TEAM OF 6Winner picks one of three

First serve, court end, or lineup advantage (the other team declares its lineup first). The other team gets the remaining two.

What must be declared in a Team of 6 lineup?

The team required to declare first submits all six players, Pair 1–3 order, and every player's Odd/Even designation. The other team then declares its lineup. Once both are submitted, the lineups are locked for that match.

Round-robin ranking

Standings & Tiebreakers

Round-robin standings determine advancement and playoff seeding. Apply each criterion in order only when teams remain tied.

1
Highest Win RateMatches won ÷ matches played.
2
Highest Points Earned (PE)Total round-robin points scored.
3
Lowest Points Conceded (PC)Fewer opponent points ranks higher.
4
Highest Points Quotient (PQ)PQ = PE ÷ (PE + PC).
What if teams are still tied?

For exactly two tied teams, the head-to-head winner ranks higher. For three or more tied teams, PE, PC, then PQ are recalculated using only matches among the tied teams. If a complete tie remains, Match Control conducts a final draw in the presence of team representatives.

Fine print

Important to Know

Forfeit scores, safety, score disputes, and who has the final say.

Forfeit Scores

Pre-match: Active/Leisure round robin or semifinal = 11–0; medal match = 15–0; Team of 6 = 31–0. In-progress: winner receives the target score; losing team keeps its actual score.

Medical / Safety

A player who cannot safely continue must notify the official or Match Control. Roster/lineup changes after the match starts require Match Control approval for a verified medical or safety reason.

Score Confirmation

Raise scoring/rule disputes immediately, preferably before the next rally. Both teams must confirm the final score before leaving the court.

Standard Rules

Unless modified in this guide, standard tournament pickleball rules apply, including legal serves, the two-bounce rule, non-volley-zone restrictions, faults and line calls.

Match Control

Match Control has final authority on tournament administration, schedule adjustments, and interpretation of Dinkabalen-specific rules.
